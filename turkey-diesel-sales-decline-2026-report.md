# The Decline of Diesel (Motorin) Sales in Turkey — 2026 Analysis Report

*Prepared: 23 July 2026*
*Scope: Turkish road-fuel market, with focus on diesel demand for a fuel distribution company*
*Primary sources: EPDK petroleum-market sector reports, TÜİK (Turkish Statistical Institute), PETDER, ODMD/ODD, TAİD, IEA — figures triangulated via multiple press reports of the official bulletins where direct access was restricted. See Sources and Data Caveats.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Turkey's diesel (motorin) sales have rolled over in 2026 after a record 2025. Cumulative January–May 2026 diesel sales are down roughly **−3% year-on-year (~9.9 Mt vs ~10.2 Mt)**, but the decline is accelerating sharply: **April −4.1%, May −15.7% y/y** — while gasoline sales keep growing at double digits (**+~10% Jan–May 2026**). This is a *diesel-specific* decline, not a general fuel-demand collapse.

The decline is best understood as **three stacked layers**:

1. **An acute 2026 shock** — the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption from late February 2026 produced the largest supply disruption in oil-market history (IEA), hitting middle distillates hardest. Turkish pump diesel briefly exceeded **80 TL/L in March 2026** and is back above **74–75 TL/L in July** despite state tax support. Regional trade and the Iran/Iraq trucking corridors were disrupted, and Turkish hauliers lost corridor share to Iranian-plated trucks that refuel with subsidized Iranian diesel.
2. **A cyclical layer** — the orthodox disinflation program (policy rate 37%, three straight holds citing the war) is cooling exactly the diesel-intensive sectors: industry contracted −0.8% in Q1 2026, exports slumped, and agriculture — after farm GDP fell −8.8% in 2025 on drought and frost — cut planted area, reducing tractor fuel use.
3. **A structural layer** — the end of dieselization. Diesel's share of new passenger-car sales collapsed from **~62% in 2015 to 6.2% in H1 2026** (historic low). Hybrids + EVs took **>51% of new-car sales in 2026** for the first time. The diesel car park share peaked at **38.3% in 2020** and is down to **32.1% (June 2026)**, eroding ~1.5 pp/year. This layer guarantees the decline does not fully reverse even if prices normalize.

**Key forward risk:** the eşel-mobil ÖTV support that cushioned pump prices during the war steps down to 25% in August–September and **ends entirely on 1 October 2026**, implying a further pump-price step-up and renewed demand pressure in Q4 2026.

## 2. What the Official Data Shows

### 2.1 Annual diesel sales (EPDK)

| Year | Motorin sales | YoY | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 24.54 Mt | −1.6% | EPDK annual report |
| 2023 | ~26.1 Mt | ~+6% | derived; strong growth year |
| 2024 | ~26.1 Mt | ~0–1% | flat (PETDER: ~31.7M m³, +0.1%) |
| 2025 | **27.08 Mt** | **+3.7%** | record; flattered by discounted Russian-origin diesel and tax-suppressed prices |

2025 was a three-year record for the whole fuel market (34.49 Mt total, +5.3%), with diesel ~78% of the barrel. The 2026 decline therefore comes off a high base.

### 2.2 Monthly 2026 — the turn (EPDK monthly reports)

| Month 2026 | Motorin | YoY | Benzin | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | 1,861,387 t | **−1.7%** | 428,812 t | +9.1% |
| February | 1,834,490 t | +1.1% | 417,803 t | +17.7% |
| March | 2,260,390 t | +8.1%* | 480,187 t | +8.1% |
| April | 2,004,871 t | **−4.1%** | 466,449 t | +6.1% |
| May | 1,960,637 t | **−15.7%** | 521,965 t | **+11.4%** |

\* March's jump reflects panic/stock buying ahead of war-driven price increases plus Ramadan-holiday timing; the underlying trend resumes in April–May. The May print also includes a high May-2025 base effect — the underlying cumulative trend is ~−3% with accelerating Q2 weakness.

Corroborating signals:
- **Diesel-type imports collapsed every month of 2026** (Feb −30.9%, Apr −26.2%, May −27.0%) while crude imports rose — refineries substituted for imported product, and distributors bought less overall.
- May 2026 total petroleum sales fell −10.5% y/y; diesel was the entire drag.
- Refinery diesel *production* also fell (Feb −9.7%) while total output rose — refiners are pivoting yields toward gasoline and jet (jet exports +33.7% y/y in May).

### 2.3 The gasoline–diesel crossover

Gasoline has nearly **doubled since 2022** (3.30 Mt → 5.69 Mt in 2025, +16.4% in 2025 alone) while diesel grew only ~10% over the same span. The benzin:motorin ratio moved from ~1:7.4 (2022) to ~1:4.3 (early 2026). Every month of 2026 shows the same pattern: **gasoline up, diesel down/flat.** Hybrids burn gasoline — the fleet transition (Section 3) directly feeds this divergence.

### 2.4 LPG (otogaz) — also in structural decline

2025 LPG sales: 3.95 Mt, **−4.7%** (otogaz −4.8%). LPG's share of the car park fell from 6.7% of conversions-era peaks to ~1.0% of new sales; the parc share dropped from 36.7% (2020) to 29.4% (June 2026). Both legacy alternative fuels — diesel and LPG — are losing to gasoline-hybrid and electric.

The erosion is accelerating (~1.5 pp/year now vs ~0.7 pp/year in 2020–22). The diesel car stock is ageing and shrinking by attrition, because almost no diesel cars enter the fleet anymore:

### 3.2 New passenger-car sales by fuel — flow (ODMD)

| Fuel | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gasoline | 66.8% | ~60% | 47.0% | ~42% |
| Hybrid | 10.8% | 18.8% | 27.2% | ~32% |
| Electric (BEV) | 7.5% | 10.7% | 17.7% | 18.5% |
| **Diesel** | **13.8%** | **9.8%** | **7.4%** | **6.2% (historic low)** |
| LPG | 1.1% | ~0.5% | 0.7% | ~0.6% |

For historical contrast: diesel was **61.7% of new car sales in 2015**. Diesel car unit sales fell −32.7% y/y in Jan–Apr 2026. In 2026 hybrids+EVs passed 50% of new-car sales for the first time (51.2–51.6% H1). BEV park passed 450,000 by mid-2026 (from 373,733 at end-2025); public charge points reached 45,660 (from ~12,000 at start-2024), with H1 2026 charging energy +153% y/y.

The overall market is also contracting: after three record years (2023–2025, peaking at 1,368,400 car+LCV in 2025), **H1 2026 sales fell −8.2% y/y** (June alone −11.4%).

### 3.3 Commercial vehicles — the core of motorin demand

Passenger cars are a minority of diesel consumption; trucks, vans, buses and tractors dominate. There:
- **Light commercial (LCV/kamyonet):** record 283,904 units in 2025 (+10%), but **H1 2026 −1.7%**, and June 2026 alone **−15.5% y/y** — "commercial slammed the brakes" (Dünya). The segment remains effectively all-diesel, so LCV sales are a leading indicator for motorin.
- **Heavy commercial (TAİD):** 38,957 units in 2025 (tractor-units −6%); **Q1 2026 a "controlled contraction."** The heavy fleet isn't shrinking, but new investment is soft — consistent with weak freight activity.

**Implication:** passenger-segment diesel demand is in structural decline; total motorin demand is increasingly a pure freight + agriculture play — and both of those are weak in 2026 for cyclical reasons.

## 4. Why Diesel Is Falling — The Drivers, Ranked

### Layer 1 — Acute: the 2026 Iran war fuel-price shock *(strong evidence; explains the timing and depth)*

- The US–Israel–Iran war from late February 2026 and the Strait of Hormuz disruption (~20% of world oil trade) caused what the IEA calls the **largest supply disruption in oil-market history**. Brent rose ~65% in March (to ~$109–110/bbl); global Q2 supply fell ~6.9 mb/d y/y.
- The shock was **diesel-skewed**: Gulf export refineries offline plus middle-distillate stocks near 20-year lows pushed diesel cracks far above gasoline. European diesel hit ~€2.08/L (+30%).
- **Turkish pump transmission:** diesel briefly >80 TL/L in March; after the 17 June de-escalation it fell to ~64.7 TL/L, then renewed strikes and July hikes (+8.5 TL cumulative in July) took it back above 74–75 TL/L.
- IEA now forecasts the **first annual decline in world oil demand since 2020** — Turkey's diesel drop has a global demand-destruction backdrop.
- The sales data match the timeline exactly: pre-buying in March (+8.1%), then −4.1% April, −15.7% May.

### Layer 2 — Cyclical: macro cooling and freight weakness *(strong evidence on facts; entangled with Layer 1)*

- **Tight money:** policy rate 37%, with TCMB pausing cuts for three straight meetings (Mar–Jun 2026) explicitly citing the war's inflation risk. Q1 2026 GDP +2.5% (below consensus), with **industry contracting −0.8%** while household consumption (+4.8%) did the lifting — precisely the mix that lifts gasoline and depresses diesel.
- **Freight/corridors:** Jan–May 2026 Turkish-plated truck runs to Iran fell −17% on exports and **−48% on imports**, while Iranian-plated runs grew — every shifted trip is diesel bunkered in Iran (heavily subsidized), not bought in Turkey. Border waits reached 28 days; exporters reported ~$815M of regional export losses in a single war month; war-risk premiums tripled sea-freight rates.
- **Agriculture:** farm GDP −8.8% in 2025; first-harvest wheat yields −40–50% on drought/frost; ~900,000 dekar of farmland damaged by disasters in H1 2026 (TZOB); reduced planting = fewer tractor-hours. Agriculture is roughly low-teens % of diesel use.

### Layer 3 — Structural: the end of dieselization *(strong evidence; slow but irreversible)*

- Diesel lost its historical pump-price advantage: diesel now prices **~4–5 TL/L above gasoline** (74–75 vs ~70 TL/L, July 2026) — a complete reversal of the relationship that built Turkey's diesel car park. The lower ÖTV on diesel is now fully offset by diesel's higher ex-refinery cost ("Turkey's diesel tax buffer evaporates" — bne IntelliNews).
- Automakers are dropping diesel variants; Euro 7 pressure; municipalities discussing old-diesel restrictions; only 53% of Turkish consumers prefer ICE for their next car (Deloitte 2026).
- Mirrored Europe-wide: EU diesel-car share of new sales fell ~49% → ~9% in a decade; EU oil demand −270 kb/d in 2025.
- Rail: 2026 declared "railway year," rail freight share targeted 6.76% → 7.53% — marginal near-term diesel displacement.

### Secondary / monitoring items *(partial or speculative)*

- **Illegal fuel ("10 numara yağ"):** major June 2026 operation put 10 fuel companies under TMSF trusteeship; İstanbul seized 242,500 L in Q1 alone. Two-way effect: crackdowns raise recorded volumes, but 75–80 TL/L diesel raises the incentive for untaxed fuel, which would depress *recorded* sales. Unquantified — monitor.
- **Supply-side reshuffle:** US sanctions on Rosneft/Lukoil (Nov 2025) + EU ban on fuels from Russian crude (Jan 2026) removed the discounted Russian diesel that flattered 2025 volumes and raised feedstock costs. Note: Tüpraş's *domestic diesel sales rose +20% y/y in Q1 2026* — it is taking share from imports even as total demand falls. Distributor-level declines and refinery-level gains coexist; don't confuse the signals.
- **Biodiesel/ethanol blending:** small volume effects (~0.5% obligation); gasoline ethanol actually cut 2% → 1.5% for 2026.

## 5. "What Else Can It Be?" — Checklist for Your Analysis

You asked what to analyze beyond the fleet's fuel/vehicle distribution. Based on this research, a complete diagnostic for a distributor should cover:

1. **Fleet fuel mix (stock + flow)** — TÜİK park data *and* ODMD new-sales mix (Section 3). The flow matters more than the stock for the trend.
2. **Fuel volumes by product** — EPDK monthly sector reports (motorin vs benzin vs otogaz), plus the import/production split.
3. **Relative pump prices** — the diesel-vs-gasoline gap and the *real* (inflation-adjusted) diesel price to hauliers/farmers; ÖTV indexation (Jan/July) and eşel-mobil timeline.
4. **Freight activity** — UND corridor statistics (especially Iran/Iraq), KGM ton-km and traffic counts, export volumes (TİM), container/sea-freight rates. Diesel is a freight fuel first.
5. **Macro cycle** — industrial production, construction activity, GDP composition (consumption vs industry), TCMB rate path.
6. **Agriculture** — planted area, drought/frost damage (TZOB), mazot support payments.
7. **Substitution technologies** — hybrid/EV sales and charging-network throughput (EPDK: H1 2026 charging energy +153% y/y), rail freight share.
8. **Base effects** — 2025 was a record year inflated by cheap Russian-origin diesel and suppressed prices; y/y comparisons in 2026 are against a high base.
9. **Illegal/untaxed fuel intensity** — correlates with real price peaks; distorts *recorded* sales.
10. **Your own channel mix** — a distributor's decline can be market share loss to refinery-direct sales (Tüpraş domestic diesel +20% in Q1 2026), not just market contraction. Separate the two.

## 6. Outlook and Implications for a Fuel Distributor

- **Q3–Q4 2026:** eşel-mobil support steps down (25% Aug–Sep) and **ends 1 October 2026**; when international prices fall, ÖTV is raised to recapture the decline. Either way, pump diesel stays high → continued demand pressure. Renewed Iran-related supply risk keeps distillate cracks elevated.
- **Structural floor:** even after prices normalize, the fleet math is one-way. Diesel car inflow ≈ 6% of sales vs a 32% park share means the passenger diesel park shrinks for years. Expect passenger-related motorin demand to decline a few percent per year regardless of the cycle.
- **Where diesel demand lives now:** kamyon/kamyonet/bus/tractor. Track LCV and heavy-truck sales (both contracting in 2026) and freight corridors as your leading indicators.
- **Where growth lives:** gasoline (+16% in 2025, +10% in 2026 YTD) — driven by hybrids — and electric charging (sessions +97%, energy +153% y/y). Station-network product mix, hybrid-friendly forecourt strategy, and charging infrastructure are the offsets to a shrinking diesel barrel.
- **Scenario framing for 2026 full year:** if June–December runs at April–May's pace (−4% to −16%), full-year 2026 motorin could land roughly **−4% to −7% below 2025** (~25.2–26.0 Mt vs 27.08 Mt) — worse if the eşel-mobil exit bites in Q4, better if the war de-escalates durably. Treat as a planning range, not a forecast.

## 7. Data Caveats

1. Direct access to data.tuik.gov.tr, epdk.gov.tr, petder.org.tr and odmd.org.tr PDFs was blocked in this research environment; official-bulletin figures were triangulated across ≥2 independent press reports each. **Verify key figures against the primary PDFs before external use:** EPDK *Petrol Piyasası Aylık Sektör Raporu*, EPDK annual reports, TÜİK *Motorlu Kara Taşıtları* bulletins, ODMD monthly press bulletins, PETDER sector reports.
2. 2021/2023/2024 diesel tonnages marked "derived" are back-calculated from EPDK YoY percentages.
3. Jan–May 2026 cumulative totals are sums of monthly EPDK prints, not an official cumulative figure.
4. PETDER reports volumes in m³ (diesel ~0.83 t/m³), EPDK in tonnes — never mix bases.
5. ODMD fuel-type splits cover passenger cars only; the LCV segment is effectively all-diesel but not broken out.
6. June 2026 EPDK data was not yet published at compile time (~7-week lag).
